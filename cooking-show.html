A Live 5-Course Cooking Show in Your Kitchen.

A dinner your friends will keep talking about.

Chef Alecia arrives two or three hours before your guests, unpacks, and sets the ingredients out on the counter where everyone will be able to see them: the scotch bonnets, the ginger, the fish, shrimp, chicken and whatever the season hands her that week.

She gets to work and people drift in to watch. The show hasn’t begun, but watching the prep with a glass of wine is half the fun. The smells begin to take over the kitchen, and it draws everyone closer. Then it’s time to sit. She cooks five courses over about three hours and talks the whole way through it. Where the dish comes from. Why it’s made that way. What her grandmother did differently, and why. Throughout the meal there is laughter, questions, jokes and somebody usually ends up at the counter with a spoon in their hand.

The fruit flambé is lit in front of you — a perfect ending.

This is what she loves to do. It was in Negril for twenty years, and now it’s here for you.

How an evening runs

Four beats.

  1. She arrives two to three hours ahead.

    Dinner shows start at 6:00 pm. She arrives two to three hours ahead to set up, depending on the size of your party. Earlier works too — just tell us on the form.

  2. Guests sit at the counter or the table.

    Whichever puts them closest. She wants to be able to hand plates directly. Wine glasses full. Music low.

  3. Five courses come out over three hours.

    Soup, salad or an appetizer, a fish course, the main, and dessert. She talks while she cooks. Why those two spices belong together. What the pimento is doing besides tasting good. What her grandmother did differently.

  4. She cleans the kitchen.

    You do not touch a dish. She leaves it the way she found it.
