The Cooking Class

Learn to cook and spice like a Jamaican.

You’ll crack a coconut and make the cream yourself — the real one, not the can. That’s where the flavor comes from, and it’s the first thing most people have never done. You’ll press the juice too, from whatever’s ripe that week.

Then Chef Alecia sets everyone up with a job. You chop, you season, you taste as you go. She teaches the spice as you work — why those two belong together, why the pimento goes in when it does, what the ginger is doing besides tasting good. She learned all of it standing beside her grandmother over a woodfire, and none of it was ever written down.

Three hours, from 11:00 to 2:00. At the end you sit down together to a three-course lunch you made yourself — and you leave knowing how to make it again.

Good for a girls’ weekend, a family in town, a birthday, or a team that’s tired of restaurants.

How the three hours run

Coconut. Juice. Lunch.

  1. 11:00 — Crack the coconut.

    Whole coconut, machete, the way she does it in Negril. Fresh coconut water in the glass. The meat gets grated for later.

  2. 11:30 — Press the juice.

    Whatever is in season — soursop, June plum, sorrel, ginger, cucumber, tamarind. Real fruit, no simple syrup.

  3. 12:00 — Cook the three courses.

    Starter, main, dessert. Every guest is on a station. Chef Alecia moves between you, correcting technique, tasting, adjusting seasoning.

  4. 1:15 — Sit at the table.

    Eat what you made. Ask questions. Get the recipe cards to take home.

  5. 2:00 — Done.

    Kitchen clean, leftovers boxed. You have three recipes you actually know how to cook now.

Technique you take home

What you’ll actually learn.

  • Jerk from scratch

    The rub, the wet marinade, the smoke. Why almost every jerk you have had in the US is off, and how to fix it in your own oven.

  • Real curry

    Blooming the spice, not dumping powder in a sauce. Curried chicken, curry shrimp, curry chickpea — same technique, different protein.

  • Rice and peas

    The coconut, the thyme, the scotch bonnet whole and pulled at the right moment. This is the dish most people ruin first.

  • Callaloo

    How to cook a leafy green so it does not taste like a leafy green. Also good for spinach, kale, mustard greens.

  • Fresh juices and teas

    Sorrel, tamarind, ginger, cucumber-mint. What you can grow in Florida. What you can only get frozen.

  • Scotch bonnet, respectfully

    The whole pepper, no seeds. When to leave it in and when to pull it out. Nobody is running to the sink for water.

Frequently asked

The questions people actually ask.

How is a class different from the cooking show?

At the class, you do the work. She guides. At the live cooking show, she cooks and you watch, and it can seat up to thirty. Same food, different level of hands-on.

How many people can join a class?

Up to ten guests. $150 per guest, $750 minimum. Larger groups than ten do not work for the class format — look at the cooking show for anything bigger.

When does the class run?

Eleven to two, three hours, weekday or weekend. Crack a coconut, press fresh juice, cook a three-course lunch, sit and eat.

Do you leave recipes?

Yes. Printed recipe cards for each dish, so you can make it again the next week without guessing.

What do you provide vs what do we provide?

She brings ingredients, spice mixes, aprons, coconuts, and recipe cards. You provide the kitchen, a stove, and the people.
