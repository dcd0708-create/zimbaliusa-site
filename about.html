About Chef Alecia

I learned to cook before I learned to read.

Founding chef of Zimbali Retreats and Zimbali’s Mountain Cooking Studio — Negril, Jamaica, 2005 to 2025. Author of Forgotten Wisdoms: Ancient African Traditions for Modern Moms. Mother of four. Now cooking in Sarasota.

I grew up in the hills of Jamaica, raised by my grandparents on a family farm outside Negril. We cooked over woodfire before we had a stove. The pot was heavy, the pumpkin was picked that morning, and there were always more mouths than we thought there would be. Nobody left hungry. That is the part I kept.

Before the kitchen was a career I was a nationwide teenage track star. I mention that only because people ask why I still move fast in the kitchen at 3 a.m. of a wedding week — I have been fast since I was fifteen.

Zimbali

In 2005 I founded Zimbali Retreats in Negril with my husband Mark. It was a small mountain restaurant with a working farm behind it, forty minutes uphill from the beach where the cruise buses stopped. People had to want to come. They did.

Over the next twenty years the restaurant was ranked #1 in Jamaica on Tripadvisor for five straight years, held a 4.8 rating on 836 reviews, and won the Observer Food Award for Westmoreland in 2015. Thrillist, ELLE, USA Today, HuffPost, Culture Trip, and Islands all wrote about it. Virgin Atlantic filmed there. So did Smile Jamaica, the Gleaner, and BeachBox. But the two things I am proudest of are less flashy.

The plants

I planted more than two thousand fruit trees on that property. Mango, breadfruit, ackee, guava, June plum, star apple, otaheite apple, sweetsop, soursop. I ran the vegetable beds myself. Callaloo, cho-cho, scotch bonnet, thyme, scallion, tomato. Most of what came out of the kitchen had been growing a few hundred feet from the pot. That was the point.

The book

I published Forgotten Wisdoms: Ancient African Traditions for Modern Moms in 2025. It is a collection of what my grandmother taught me — food, herbs, remedies, rhythms of the household — written for women trying to raise children without losing themselves. You can find it on Amazon, and there is more at forgottenwisdoms.com.

Sarasota

Hurricane Melissa closed the Zimbali chapter in Jamaica in October 2025. My family and I landed in Sarasota shortly after, with the same knives and the same recipes. The food does not change because the address did.

If you want to sit at a counter and watch me cook a five-course Jamaican dinner in your own kitchen, tell me about your evening. I’ll come cook it.
