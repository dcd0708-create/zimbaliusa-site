Why a Jamaican chef

The food most private chefs don’t know how to cook.

Sarasota has a lot of private chefs. Most of them cook Mediterranean, or steakhouse, or coastal-American. Chef Alecia cooks curried goat that takes four hours, jerk with the char it is supposed to have, escovitch snapper under pickled peppers. She has done this every night for twenty years.

She can also cook a Gulf snapper straight, or a filet, or a roast chicken with the pan sauce. But most people book her because they want food they cannot easily get anywhere else on the coast.

Frequently asked

The questions people actually ask.

What’s the difference between this and the cooking show?

The cooking show is performed in front of your guests — it is the entertainment. The private chef dinner is quieter. She cooks in the kitchen and plates each course like a restaurant. Same cooking, different room.

Can she cook for a whole vacation week?

Yes — three night minimum. Dinners every night, sometimes lunches too. Send the dates and how many people and we’ll build a plan for the week.

What kind of kitchen does she need?

A stove, a sink, oven, and counter space. Rented houses on the beach are fine. She brings her own knives and pots.

Does she shop for the groceries?

Yes. Food is sourced, prepped, and cooked. You do not shop.

Are the plates cleared?

Yes. She serves and clears. Kitchen is left clean.

How much notice does she need?

Two weeks is comfortable. She sometimes takes less on quieter weeks. Short-notice bookings (inside 48 hours) are paid in full at booking and non-refundable — she has already started shopping.

Do the breakfast and lunch add-ons apply to a single night?

Yes. Breakfast is +$45 per guest, lunch is +$40 per guest, on top of the dinner rate. Add either or both to any night, including a one-night booking.

What if the group is larger than twelve?

Thirteen to twenty guests bumps the rate to $200 per guest and includes a second pair of hands in the kitchen so service stays sharp.
